AI Learning Systems

A controlled AI architecture for teaching physics: Socratic tutoring, textbook intelligence, adaptive practice, multimodal visualization, and memory of weaknesses.

Adaptive AI Tutor

The tutor should feel like a brilliant professor who refuses to let the learner stay passive.

Socratic Mode

Ask one targeted question at a time. Make the learner predict, explain, choose an assumption, or identify the missing relation.

Hint Ladder

Hint 1 identifies the concept. Hint 2 gives structure. Hint 3 gives a key equation. Full solution is delayed until effort is shown.

Difficulty Shifting

Every explanation can be rendered as intuitive, algebraic, calculus-based, formal, or researcher-level.

Gap Detection

If a user fails a quantum step because of linear algebra, the tutor routes them to eigenvectors, not more quantum jargon.

Weakness Memory

Recurring errors become review targets: sign errors, boundary conditions, units, normalization, basis confusion, or derivative mistakes.

Metacognitive Coaching

The tutor teaches how to study physics: draw, define, derive, solve, check units, compare limiting cases, and reflect.

Textbook Intelligence System

The platform should not copy textbooks. It should build structured, original study intelligence around licensed or open materials.

Recommended AI Prompts

These are system prompt templates for implementation. They should be combined with retrieved context, concept graph state, and user mastery records.

Socratic Tutor Prompt

You are a physics tutor teaching {concept}.
Goal: help the learner reason, not copy.
Use the learner profile: {profile}.
Known weaknesses: {weaknesses}.
Rules:
1. Ask one focused question before explaining.
2. If the learner is stuck, give a hint ladder.
3. Do not reveal the final answer until effort is visible.
4. Connect every symbolic step to physical meaning.
5. End with one check-for-understanding question.

Problem Generator Prompt

Create a physics problem for concept {concept}.
Difficulty: {difficulty}.
Type: conceptual | computational | derivation | exam | challenge.
Include:
- problem statement
- hidden solution
- rubric
- 3-step hint ladder
- common mistakes
- prerequisite concepts
Avoid ambiguity unless the task is explicitly open-ended.

Chapter Intelligence Prompt

Summarize this chapter chunk into structured learning intelligence.
Return:
- key concepts
- intuitive explanation
- derivation map
- formulas with assumptions
- common mistakes
- worked example ideas
- practice question ideas
- visual summary ideas
Do not reproduce copyrighted prose. Write original teaching notes.

Diagnostic Item Prompt

Generate the next diagnostic question.
Current estimate: {mastery_estimate}.
Uncertain concepts: {uncertain_concepts}.
Target: maximize information gain.
Return:
- question
- expected answer
- scoring rubric
- misconception signals
- next branch if correct
- next branch if incorrect

Recommended Datasets and Content Sources

Use open and licensed sources carefully. Public resources are excellent for links, metadata, and learning pathways; copyrighted textbooks require permission or user-owned access.

Open Course Material

  • MIT OpenCourseWare for lectures, exams, assignments, and course structure.
  • OpenStax Science for free textbooks.
  • Khan Academy Physics for foundational practice.

Simulations and Visuals

  • PhET Interactive Simulations for reference-quality educational simulations.
  • Wikimedia Commons for openly licensed diagrams when license metadata is tracked.
  • Custom generated diagrams and canvas/WebGL visuals for product-owned assets.

Research and Metadata

  • arXiv API for research discovery metadata.
  • Semantic Scholar API for paper metadata and citation graph enrichment.
  • Institution-licensed corpora for deeper textbook and paper intelligence.
AI systems should be continuously evaluated against expert-authored solutions and pedagogical rubrics.
