User Journey

Every screen should answer one question: what should I learn next, why, and how do I prove I learned it?

Arrival

The learner chooses a goal

Examples: prepare for a physics master’s, learn quantum computing, repair calculus, or reach research literacy.

Diagnostic

The AI maps current knowledge

Adaptive questions determine math level, physics intuition, reasoning profile, and learning preferences.

Roadmap

The platform builds a path

The user receives a dependency-aware plan with time estimates, first lesson, and skipped topics explained.

Lesson Loop

Learn, simulate, practice, reflect

The lesson workspace combines explanation, derivation, visualization, Socratic tutor, and problems.

Mastery Update

The system updates the graph

Attempts, tutor conversations, and exam results update mastery and schedule review.

Achievement

Certificates and research path

Learners can earn certificates, enter advanced tracks, join cohorts, or start research-guided projects.

Key Product States

These states matter because learning is emotional. The product should handle confusion, momentum, failure, and mastery with care.

Stuck

Tutor gives hint ladder, suggests prerequisite review, and offers a simpler analogy.

Overconfident

System asks transfer problems and conceptual explanations before unlocking advanced topics.

Returning After Break

Dashboard shows a short repair path and one confidence-building review set.

Exam Ready

Exam hall opens timed practice and shows topic-by-topic readiness.

Mastered

Concept unlocks advanced applications, simulations, and challenge problems.

Research Curious

Research mode connects the concept to papers, open questions, and computational projects.

The user journey is mastery-based: no shame, no guessing, no passive video bingeing.
